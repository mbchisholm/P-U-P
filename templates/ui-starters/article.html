Open with the answer. The first paragraph should tell a hurried reader everything; the rest of the page is for the reader who wants to know why.

The core idea

Body text lives inside a measure of roughly 65 characters — that's the max-width: 680px on this column, and it's doing more for readability than any font choice could. Lines longer than that make the eye lose its place on the way back; lines much shorter make reading feel choppy.

Paragraphs get space between them, not indentation, and headings sit closer to the text they introduce than to the text they follow. That one asymmetry is most of what people mean by "clean."

Pull one sentence out when it carries the whole section — sparingly, or it stops meaning anything.

Showing code

Code blocks sit in a quiet panel and scroll horizontally rather than wrapping:

$ curl -s https://api.example.com/status
{ "ok": true, "uptime": "99.99%" }

When to use lists

Use a list when the items are genuinely parallel:

  • Each item is one idea, phrased the same way.
  • Three to five items — more than that wants subheadings.
  • If the items need full paragraphs, they're sections, not bullets.
