A soil moisture sensor that texts me

#sensors #esp32

// contents

  • The idea
  • Reading the probe
  • What broke

My monstera dies quietly. By the time the leaves droop it's been dry for a week. So: a capacitive soil probe, an ESP32, and a webhook that texts me before the plant starts suffering in silence.

The idea

Read the probe every hour, keep a short rolling average, and only message me on a trend — a single dry reading is noise, six in a row is a thirsty plant. The whole loop is about forty lines of C++.

Rule of thumb from this build: alert on trends, never on single sensor readings.

Reading the probe

The probe outputs an analog voltage that drops as soil moisture rises. Calibration is two measurements — dry air and a glass of water — and a map() between them:

const int DRY = 2590;   // probe in air
const int WET = 1180;   // probe in water

int pct = map(analogRead(PIN), DRY, WET, 0, 100);
pct = constrain(pct, 0, 100);

What broke

Powering the probe continuously corrodes it in weeks. The fix is to feed it from a GPIO pin and only power it up for the 50ms around each reading. Electrolysis doesn't take days off; neither should your pull-request reviews.

Parts: ESP32 dev board, capacitive soil sensor v1.2, one stubborn houseplant.
